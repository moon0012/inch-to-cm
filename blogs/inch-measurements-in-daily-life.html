10 Everyday Things You Didn't Know Were Measured in Inches

Inches are hiding in plain sight all around you. From the phone in your pocket to the pizza on your plate, here are ten surprising places where this ancient unit of measurement still rules.

Published on April 8, 2026 · 7 min read

Inches Are Everywhere

You might think of inches as something you only encounter when buying lumber or measuring your height. But this 2.54-centimeter unit of length shows up in far more places than you'd expect. Even in countries that officially use the metric system, inches quietly dominate entire industries.

Here are ten everyday things measured in inches — and what those measurements actually mean in centimeters. You'll never look at these objects the same way again.

1

Phone Screens

When Apple announces a new iPhone with a "6.7-inch display," that number refers to the diagonal measurement of the screen. This convention dates back to the early days of television and has stuck around for every screen-based device since. Interestingly, even phone manufacturers in metric countries advertise screen sizes in inches.

A 6.7-inch phone screen measures about 17.02 cm diagonally. The iPhone 16 Pro Max, Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra, and most flagship phones hover around this size. Smaller phones like the iPhone 16 come in at 6.1 inches (15.49 cm).

6.7 inches = 17.02 cm
2

Pizza Sizes

Order a "large pizza" and you'll typically get a 14-inch pie. But did you know that pizza sizes are always measured by the diameter in inches — even in most metric countries? A small is usually 10 inches, a medium is 12, and a large is 14 to 16 inches.

Here's a fun math fact: a single 18-inch pizza has more area than two 12-inch pizzas. The area of a circle grows with the square of the radius, so that 18-inch pizza gives you 254 square inches versus 226 square inches for two 12-inchers. Always order the bigger pizza.

14-inch pizza = 35.56 cm diameter
3

TV and Monitor Screens

Like phones, TV sizes are measured diagonally in inches — a global standard that even metric-first countries follow. When you're shopping for a 55-inch TV, that diagonal measurement translates to 139.7 cm. But the actual width and height depend on the aspect ratio.

A 55-inch TV with a standard 16:9 aspect ratio is roughly 47.9 inches (121.7 cm) wide and 27.0 inches (68.6 cm) tall. Knowing both the inch and centimeter dimensions is essential when fitting a TV into a specific space. Check our conversion guides for common TV sizes.

55 inches = 139.7 cm diagonal
4

Snowfall and Rainfall

Weather reports in the US always describe precipitation in inches. "We got 8 inches of snow last night" or "2 inches of rain this week" are phrases every American knows. Meteorologists measure precipitation by collecting it in standardized rain gauges marked in inches.

One inch of rain equals 2.54 cm, and it translates to about 17,400 gallons of water per acre. One inch of snow, depending on its density, typically contains about 0.1 inches of water. In metric countries, precipitation is reported in millimeters, where 1 inch equals 25.4 mm.

1 inch of rain = 25.4 mm
5

Tire Sizes

Look at the side of any car tire and you'll see something like "225/65R17." That last number — 17 — is the wheel diameter in inches. Even though the first two numbers (tread width and aspect ratio) use the metric system, the rim diameter stubbornly remains in inches worldwide. This is true whether you're buying tires in Tokyo, Berlin, or Detroit.

A 17-inch wheel has a diameter of 43.18 cm. The mix of metric and imperial in a single tire specification is one of the quirkiest examples of measurement systems coexisting in the same product.

17-inch rim = 43.18 cm
6

Construction Nails

If you've ever visited a hardware store, you've seen nails labeled as "6d" or "16d." The "d" stands for "penny" (from the old English "denarius"), and it originally referred to the price of 100 nails. Today, it indicates the nail's length. A 6d nail is 2 inches long (5.08 cm), while a 16d nail is 3.5 inches (8.89 cm).

This centuries-old naming convention persists across the construction industry in the US, Canada, and the UK. Even modern building codes reference nail sizes using this inch-based penny system.

16d nail = 3.5 inches = 8.89 cm
7

Plumbing Pipe Diameters

Plumbing in most of North America runs on inch-based pipe sizes. A standard bathroom sink drain is 1.25 inches (3.175 cm), kitchen drains are 1.5 inches (3.81 cm), and main sewer lines are typically 4 inches (10.16 cm) in diameter.

This matters enormously if you're doing any plumbing work with parts sourced internationally. European pipes use metric dimensions, and the sizes don't always have exact imperial equivalents. A "half-inch" pipe in the US is not exactly the same as a 15mm pipe from Europe, even though they're often used interchangeably.

1.5-inch pipe = 3.81 cm
8

Waist and Clothing Sizes

In the US, waist measurements for pants are given in inches. A "32-inch waist" means the waistband measures 32 inches around (81.28 cm). Inseam lengths — the measurement from crotch to hem — also use inches. A 30-inch inseam is 76.2 cm.

European clothing typically uses centimeters, which is why international shoppers frequently need to convert between systems. A European size 50 jacket roughly corresponds to a US 40 (measured in inches across the chest). Our converter tool makes these translations quick and painless.

32-inch waist = 81.28 cm
9

Paper Sizes (US Letter vs. A4)

The standard US letter paper size is 8.5 x 11 inches (21.59 x 27.94 cm). The rest of the world uses A4, which measures 210 x 297 mm (8.27 x 11.69 inches). The two sizes are close but not identical, which causes countless printing headaches when sharing documents internationally.

US Legal paper is even longer at 8.5 x 14 inches (21.59 x 35.56 cm). The US paper size system is based on inches, while the international A-series is based on the metric system with a clever mathematical property: each size is exactly half the area of the next larger size.

8.5 x 11 inches = 21.59 x 27.94 cm
10

Hard Drives and SSDs

Storage drives are categorized by their physical size in inches. A standard laptop SSD is a "2.5-inch drive" (6.35 cm), while desktop hard drives are "3.5-inch" (8.89 cm). The compact M.2 SSD form factor is measured in millimeters (22mm wide), showing the gradual shift toward metric in newer technologies.

This inch-based sizing convention has been standard in the computer industry since the first personal computers in the 1980s. Even manufacturers in Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan — all metric countries — label their drives using inch dimensions because that's what the global market expects.

2.5-inch drive = 6.35 cm

The Takeaway

Inches are far more embedded in global culture than most people realize. From the tech in your pocket to the food on your table, from the pipes in your walls to the tires on your car, this ancient unit of measurement remains surprisingly relevant in the modern world.
