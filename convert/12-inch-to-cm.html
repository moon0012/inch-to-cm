12 Inches to Centimeters

12 inches = 30.48 cm
Also known as: 1 foot
Formula: 12 × 2.54 = 30.48 cm

Why 12 Inches (1 Foot) Is a Cornerstone Measurement

Twelve inches make one foot, and at 30.48 centimeters, this conversion is arguably the most important in the imperial system. The foot serves as the base unit for architecture, construction, and real estate across the United States and several other countries. Every building blueprint, room dimension, and property listing in these markets uses feet as the primary unit.

The 12-inch ruler is the first measuring tool most students encounter in school. Its metric equivalent of 30.48 cm is close to the 30 cm rulers used in metric countries, which is why the two systems feel familiar to international travelers. Vinyl record collectors know this measurement intimately: the classic LP record has a diameter of 12 inches (30.48 cm), a format that has seen a remarkable resurgence in the streaming era.

In the food world, the "footlong" submarine sandwich is a cultural icon in North America, measuring exactly 12 inches. Standard bookshelf depths, laptop screen sizes around 12-13 inches, and common shelving board widths all orbit this dimension. For furniture builders, 12-inch (30.48 cm) shelf depth is a common specification that accommodates most paperback and hardcover books.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many centimeters is 12 inches (1 foot)?
12 inches, or 1 foot, equals exactly 30.48 centimeters. This is one of the most essential imperial-to-metric conversions to memorize for everyday use.
Why is a foot 12 inches?
The 12-inch foot traces back to ancient Roman and medieval measurement systems. Twelve was favored because it is divisible by 2, 3, 4, and 6, making it practical for splitting measurements into halves, thirds, and quarters without decimals.
What common objects are 12 inches long?
Many familiar objects measure 12 inches (30.48 cm): a standard ruler, a vinyl LP record diameter, a footlong sub sandwich, the depth of standard bookshelves, and many laptop screens (measured diagonally).
