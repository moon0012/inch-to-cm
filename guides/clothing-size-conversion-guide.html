Clothing Size Conversion Guide: Inches to CM for Perfect Fit

Published April 8, 2026 · 9 min read

Why Clothing Size Conversion Matters

Online shopping has made it possible to buy clothing from anywhere in the world, but sizing systems vary dramatically between countries. A size "M" in the United States, the United Kingdom, Europe, and Asia can correspond to very different body measurements. The root of the confusion lies in the fact that some countries express body measurements in inches while others use centimeters.

Getting the conversion right is the difference between a garment that fits perfectly and one that needs to be returned. Returns for international orders are often expensive, slow, and sometimes impossible. By understanding how to convert your body measurements accurately between inches and centimeters, you can shop confidently from any retailer in the world.

How to Measure Yourself Correctly

Before converting any measurements, you need accurate numbers to work with. Here is how to take the four most important body measurements for clothing:

Chest / Bust

Wrap a soft measuring tape around the fullest part of your chest, keeping it parallel to the floor. Keep the tape snug but not tight. Breathe normally and take the measurement on an exhale. For women, measure over the fullest part of the bust while wearing a non-padded bra.

Waist

Measure around your natural waistline, which is the narrowest part of your torso. This is typically about 2.5 cm (1 inch) above your belly button. Do not suck in your stomach -- stand naturally and relaxed for an accurate reading.

Hips

Stand with your feet together and measure around the widest part of your hips and buttocks. The tape should be parallel to the floor. This is usually about 20 cm (8 inches) below your waist.

Inseam

Measure from the crotch seam of a well-fitting pair of pants down to the bottom of the leg. Alternatively, measure from your inner thigh down to the ankle bone. This measurement is especially important for pants, trousers, and jeans.

Measuring tip: Use a flexible fabric tape measure, not a metal one. If you do not have a tape measure, use a piece of string, mark the length, then measure the string against a ruler. Use our inch to cm converter for instant conversion.

Common Fitting Issues When Converting Sizes

Even with accurate conversions, there are several pitfalls that lead to poor fit when shopping internationally:

Vanity Sizing

Many US brands practice "vanity sizing," where a labeled size corresponds to larger actual measurements than the standard suggests. A pair of jeans labeled "32-inch waist" might actually measure 34 inches around. This inconsistency means you should always compare your actual body measurement in centimeters against the brand's specific size chart.

Cut and Style Differences

European clothing tends to have a slimmer, more tailored cut compared to American clothing. A European medium may have the same chest measurement as a US medium but less room through the torso and arms. Asian clothing often has shorter body lengths and narrower shoulders even at the same chest circumference.

Stretch and Fabric

Clothing made with elastane or spandex blends will stretch to accommodate a range of sizes. When a size chart shows measurements in centimeters, those typically represent the garment's unstretched dimensions. If the fabric has significant stretch, you may be able to size down.

Online Shopping Tips for International Purchases

Follow these practical steps to improve your chances of getting the right fit when buying from international retailers:

  1. Measure yourself in centimeters. Even if you normally think in inches, having your measurements in cm avoids an extra conversion step. Use our inch to cm converter to get precise values.
  2. Check the brand's size chart, not generic charts. Every brand sizes differently. Look for a size guide link on the product page and match your cm measurements directly.
  3. Read reviews for sizing feedback. Other buyers often mention if an item runs large, small, or true to size. Look for reviewers who share their body measurements.
  4. Order from retailers with free returns. When shopping internationally for the first time, choose retailers that offer hassle-free returns in case the fit is not right.
  5. Keep a personal size profile. Record your measurements in both inches and centimeters and update them annually. Body measurements change over time.

Quick reference: To convert any clothing measurement from inches to centimeters, multiply by 2.54. For example, a 34-inch waist = 34 x 2.54 = 86.36 cm. To go from cm to inches, divide by 2.54.

Getting your clothing size right across international sizing systems comes down to two things: accurate body measurements and reliable unit conversion.
