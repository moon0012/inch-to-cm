Convert Height from Inches to CM - Height Conversion Guide

Published April 8, 2026 · 8 min read

How Height Conversion Works

Height is one of the most common measurements people need to convert between imperial and metric systems. In the United States, people state their height in feet and inches -- "I'm five foot ten." In most other countries, height is given in centimeters -- "I'm 178 cm." If you travel internationally, fill out medical forms abroad, or simply want to understand heights listed on dating profiles from other countries, knowing how to convert is essential.

The process involves two steps: first convert the feet-and-inches measurement to total inches, then multiply by 2.54 to get centimeters.

Total inches = (feet × 12) + inches
Height in cm = total inches × 2.54

Example: Convert 5'10" to centimeters

Step 1: (5 × 12) + 10 = 60 + 10 = 70 inches

Step 2: 70 × 2.54 = 177.80 cm

Example: Convert 6'2" to centimeters

Step 1: (6 × 12) + 2 = 72 + 2 = 74 inches

Step 2: 74 × 2.54 = 187.96 cm

Height in Medical and Health Contexts

Your height in centimeters matters more than you might think in medical settings. Most of the world's healthcare systems record patient height in centimeters. If you visit a doctor abroad, your medical records will list your height in metric units. Even within the United States, many hospitals and clinics are transitioning to metric for clinical precision.

Why Doctors Prefer Centimeters

  • Drug dosing: Many medications, especially for children, are dosed based on body surface area, which requires height in centimeters for accurate calculation.
  • Growth charts: Pediatric growth charts from the World Health Organization use centimeters. Tracking a child's growth percentile requires consistent metric measurements.
  • Medical research: Clinical studies universally report height in centimeters, making metric the standard for comparing patient data across borders.
  • Error reduction: Metric measurements have fewer conversion steps, reducing the chance of mistakes in time-sensitive medical situations.

BMI Calculations

Body Mass Index, one of the most common health metrics, uses height in meters (not inches or centimeters). The formula is:

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)2

To use this formula, you need your height in meters. Simply take your centimeter value and divide by 100. For example, if you are 5'8" (172.72 cm), your height in meters is 1.7272 m.

BMI Example: Person who is 5'8" and weighs 154 lbs (70 kg)

Height: 5'8" = 172.72 cm = 1.7272 m

BMI = 70 ÷ (1.7272)2 = 70 ÷ 2.983 = 23.47

This falls within the "normal weight" range (18.5 - 24.9).

Note: BMI is a screening tool, not a diagnostic measure. It does not distinguish between muscle and fat mass. Athletes with significant muscle mass may have a high BMI while being perfectly healthy. Always consult a healthcare professional for a complete health assessment.

International Height Standards

How people express height varies around the world, and the differences go beyond just the units used:

  • United States: Feet and inches exclusively in everyday conversation. "I'm 5'9"." Medical records may use either system.
  • United Kingdom: A mix. Many Britons still use feet and inches casually, but official forms increasingly request centimeters.
  • Continental Europe: Centimeters only. A German would say "Ich bin 180" (meaning 180 cm). Meters are sometimes used: "1 meter 80."
  • East Asia: Centimeters. In Japan, China, and South Korea, height is always given in centimeters.
  • Australia and New Zealand: Officially metric, but many people still think in feet and inches informally.
  • India: Feet and inches remain common in daily life, even though the metric system is official.

Height Conversion in Sports

Sports organizations around the world use different conventions, which creates frequent need for height conversion.

Basketball (NBA)

The NBA lists player heights in feet and inches on its website and broadcasts. However, in 2019 the league started requiring teams to submit official measurements without shoes, changing many listed heights. International basketball (FIBA) uses centimeters. When comparing NBA and European or Asian league players, you need to convert.

Soccer (Football)

FIFA and most international football organizations use centimeters. When scouts or coaches from American soccer programs communicate with international clubs, height conversions are routine. A midfielder listed at 5'11" in the US would be recorded as 180 cm in a European club's database.

Combat Sports

Boxing, MMA, and wrestling all use height as a factor in matchmaking and analysis. The UFC lists fighters in both systems on its international site, but many regional promotions use only one. A fighter might be described as "six foot one, 185 centimeters" during an international broadcast.
