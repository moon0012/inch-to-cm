TV Size Conversion Guide: Inches to CM for Every Screen Size

Published April 8, 2026 · 8 min read

How TV Screen Sizes Are Measured

When manufacturers advertise a 55-inch TV, they are not referring to the width or height of the screen. TV sizes are always measured diagonally, from one corner of the display panel to the opposite corner. This measurement does not include the bezel or frame surrounding the screen.

This convention dates back to the earliest days of cathode-ray tube televisions, when the round tube diameter was the most natural measurement to reference. Even though screens are now flat and rectangular, the diagonal measurement has stuck as the industry standard worldwide.

Understanding this is important because a 55-inch TV is not 55 inches wide. Due to the standard 16:9 aspect ratio used by nearly all modern televisions, a 55-inch TV actually measures approximately 47.9 inches (121.7 cm) wide and 27.0 inches (68.6 cm) tall.

Key fact: TV screen sizes are always measured diagonally. To convert any TV size from inches to centimeters, multiply the diagonal measurement by 2.54.

Viewing Distance Recommendations

Choosing the right TV size is not just about the screen dimensions -- it also depends on how far you will be sitting from the television. The Society of Motion Picture and Television Engineers (SMPTE) recommends that the screen should occupy approximately 30 degrees of your field of vision for a comfortable viewing experience.

For 4K Ultra HD televisions, you can sit closer to the screen without noticing individual pixels, which means you can comfortably use a larger TV in a smaller room compared to the Full HD era.

How Screen Size Affects Room Setup

Picking a TV is not just about the screen itself. The physical dimensions of the television, including its stand or wall mount, will determine how it fits into your living space. Here are some practical considerations:

TV Stand and Furniture Width

Your TV stand or media console needs to be wider than the television. A 65-inch TV is approximately 144 cm (56.7 inches) wide, so you will want furniture that is at least 150 cm wide. Many people underestimate this and end up with a TV that hangs over the edges of their stand.

Wall Mounting Considerations

When wall-mounting a TV, measure the available wall space in centimeters. Ensure there is at least 10-15 cm of clearance on each side for a balanced look. A 75-inch TV (190.5 cm diagonal, 166 cm wide) needs a wall section that is at least 186 cm wide.

Room Size Guidelines

  • Small bedroom or kitchen (under 12 sq m): 32" to 43" (81.3 - 109.2 cm diagonal)
  • Medium bedroom or study (12-18 sq m): 43" to 55" (109.2 - 139.7 cm diagonal)
  • Living room (18-25 sq m): 55" to 75" (139.7 - 190.5 cm diagonal)
  • Large living room or home theater (over 25 sq m): 75" to 85" (190.5 - 215.9 cm diagonal)

Buying Guide for International Shoppers

If you are purchasing a television from another country or shopping on international e-commerce platforms, understanding the inch-to-centimeter conversion is essential. Here are some tips to avoid costly mistakes:

Verify the Measurement System

Some Asian and European retailers list TV sizes in centimeters rather than inches. A TV listed as "140 cm" is approximately a 55-inch model. Always check whether the listed measurement is the diagonal, width, or total unit size including the frame.

Account for Shipping Dimensions

The box a TV ships in is always larger than the TV itself. A 65-inch TV (165.1 cm diagonal) typically ships in a box that is about 160 cm wide, 95 cm tall, and 18 cm deep. Make sure this fits through your doorways and into your elevator if applicable.

Compare Across Markets

TV model numbers and sizes can vary by region. The same brand may sell a 58-inch model in one market and a 55-inch model in another. Use our inch to cm converter to quickly compare listed sizes across different retailers and regions.

Pro tip: Before buying, cut a piece of cardboard or tape an outline on your wall matching the TV's actual width and height dimensions (not the diagonal). This gives you a realistic preview of how the screen will look in your space.

Understanding the Math Behind TV Dimensions

If you want to calculate the exact width and height of any TV size yourself, here is how the math works. Modern TVs use a 16:9 aspect ratio, which means for every 16 units of width, there are 9 units of height.

Using the Pythagorean theorem with the 16:9 ratio, you can derive these formulas:

  • Width = Diagonal x 0.8716
  • Height = Diagonal x 0.4903

For example, to find the width of a 55-inch TV in centimeters: 55 x 2.54 = 139.7 cm diagonal, then 139.7 x 0.8716 = 121.8 cm wide. This matches the values in our conversion table above.
